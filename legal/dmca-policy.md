This policy explains how to tell us that content published on the Oghmere platform (the "**Service**") infringes your copyright, and what we do about it. It supplements the [Terms of Service](./terms-of-service.md) and the [Acceptable Use & AI Policy](./acceptable-use-policy.md).

We are a Canadian company (British Columbia). We respond to copyright complaints under Canada's *Copyright Act* **notice-and-notice** regime and, for content reachable from the United States, in a manner consistent with the U.S. **Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA)**.

## 1. Where to send a notice

**Copyright contact:** **legal@oghmere.com**
(Abuse and safety reports that are not copyright-related: **abuse@oghmere.com**.)

You may also submit a notice through the in-product reporting controls, or via the copyright notice form on our site. Every notice — however it arrives — is recorded and routed to a human for review.

## 2. What a notice must contain

To let us act, your notice must include **all** of the following:

   (a) your physical or electronic **signature**, and your name;
   (b) **identification of the work** you claim is infringed (or a representative list, if multiple);
   (c) **identification of the material** you claim is infringing, with enough detail for us to find it — a direct URL is best;
   (d) your **contact information** (address, telephone number, and email);
   (e) a statement that you have a **good-faith belief** that the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law; and
   (f) a statement that the information in the notice is **accurate**, and — under penalty of perjury — that you are the copyright owner or authorized to act on the owner's behalf.

**Incomplete notices delay action.** If your notice is missing required elements we may ask you for them before we can act.

**Please do not misuse this process.** Knowingly misrepresenting that material is infringing can expose you to liability for damages, including costs and legal fees.

## 3. What we do when we receive a notice

1. We record the notice and route it to a human reviewer.
2. Consistent with the Canadian notice-and-notice regime, we **forward** the notice to the account that published the material.
3. Where the notice is complete and the claim is not plainly unfounded, we may **remove, hide, or delist** the material while the claim is reviewed. Restricting content is not a determination that infringement occurred.
4. We keep a record of the notice and of what we did.

We do not act as a judge of the underlying copyright dispute. Where the parties disagree, the dispute is between them; our role is limited to the material hosted on the Service.

## 4. Counter-notice

If your material was removed or restricted and you believe that was a mistake or misidentification, you may send a **counter-notice** to **legal@oghmere.com** including:

   (a) your **signature**, name, address, and telephone number;
   (b) **identification of the material** removed and where it appeared;
   (c) a statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a **good-faith belief** the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification; and
   (d) your **consent to jurisdiction** as required by applicable law, and that you will accept service of process from the complaining party.

We may forward your counter-notice, including your contact details, to the party who sent the original notice. Where applicable law provides for it, we may restore the material after the statutory waiting period unless the complainant informs us they have sought a court order.

## 5. Repeat infringers

Consistent with the Terms and the Acceptable Use & AI Policy, we may **suspend publishing, delist an author profile, or terminate the account** of a user who repeatedly publishes infringing material. We assess this case by case, accounting for the credibility and volume of notices and any counter-notices.

You are responsible for having the rights to anything you publish to the Community Showcase — including text, images, and anything you adapted from another work. See Terms §3.8 and the Acceptable Use & AI Policy §5.2.
